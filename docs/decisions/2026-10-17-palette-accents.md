# Palette accents

**Decided:** 2026-10-17

**Choice:** Amends [the pen palette](2026-10-16-pen-palette.md) and [palette from My pens](2026-10-16-palette-from-my-pens.md) under finding F12 of [the colour and styles audit](../audits/2026-10-16-colour-styles-audit.md). Two changes in `analysis.rs`, both with the threshold `SAME_COLOUR`, a CIE 1976 colour difference ΔE*ab of 5, about two just noticeable differences (the gain a pen must earn stays 2.3, one). First, `additions` counts a built-in ink as owned when a library pen is within 5 of it, where it matched the pen's hex exactly; the pen the user has stands in for the ink, so the note no longer offers an ink that a library pen nearly duplicates. Second, `mean_error` leaves out the samples that are paper, those within 5 of white (the palette fit assumes white paper, as the Pen use colour model does; the session's paper colour is no input of any stage), and takes the mean over the rest, so `palette_error` is the mean over the inked pixels. An image with no inked sample keeps every sample, as before. `PALETTE_GAIN`, the four-ink limit and the colourfulness gate are unchanged, and so is every drawing: only the analysis moves. The audit's third idea, a round of swaps among the library's own pens after the forward selection, is not built: no case here showed the need, and it would move the palette of every image fitted to the built-in inks, which wants a measurement of its own.

**Reason:** The audit's two causes. A pen whose colour was picked by eye or taken from a maker's swatch is never the built-in ink's hex, so the exact match offered a blue pen to a user who owned one, and a library pen next to a built-in ink was judged by the threshold more harshly than the ink. And the mean weighted white paper like any colour, so on a mostly white picture a small area could not lift it by 2.3 whatever its error. Measured with the unit tests, which draw their pictures in code (before, then after):
- Dark green mountains (#08522e) and an orange sun (#f07d1a) on white, 200 by 120 px, with a library of Black, Red, Blue #1f4fb4, Yellow and Green (the built-in ink hex for Red, Yellow and Green). Before: Yellow, Green and Red at a mean ΔE of 4.10, and the note "with a blue pen the mean colour difference falls from 4.1 to 1.8". After: Yellow, Green, Red and the library's Blue at 2.92, and no note. Given the palette without the blue pen, the built-in blue still clears the 2.3 gain, so the owned test alone keeps it out of the additions.
- A black bar (10 % of the pixels) and a red patch (3 %) on white, 100 by 100 px, no library. Before: black alone, at 2.88; the red lowered the mean by under 2.3. After: Red and Black, at 5.09, the mean over the 13 % that is inked.

The audit's own logo is not in the repository, so a scan of dark greens found a picture that fails the same way (a blue pen offered to a library that has one). The error is now larger on a picture with much white paper, since it is the mean over the inked pixels; the 2.3 gain is read against that mean. The analysis snapshot of the still life and the colour-bar tests pass unchanged, the library law (`a_library_palette_uses_owned_pens`) still holds, and the gallery's stats snapshot is untouched because no drawing moves. The law checks the exact-hex form of ownership, as it cannot call the Lab conversion; the unit test checks the new one.
