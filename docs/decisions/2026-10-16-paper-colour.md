# Paper colour

**Decided:** 2026-10-16

**Choice:** The session holds a paper colour, `#rrggbb`, white by default, beside the page rather than inside `Canvas`. `Session::set_paper` sets it. The preview renderer fills the picture with it, and the drawing-rect outline and every pen layer multiply onto it, so a white paper gives the picture it gave before. The previews that use it are the GUI's, the style thumbnails, the PNG previews of the CLI and MCP, and the GUI's page. The source and travel overlays still blend normally. A preset carries it as an optional top-level `paper` key, and `PRESET_VERSION` stays 3: a preset without the key loads as white paper, and an older build drops the key with its usual warning. The GUI sets it from the Page panel, `plotline run` and `plotline analyse` take `--paper`, and MCP `set_page` takes `paper`, which, like a pen's colour, may be an ink name. SVG, JSON paths and G-code leave the paper out. The tone map, the Pen use colour model and the plot lint still assume white paper. This settles the first of the after-0.1 candidates in §12, question 8.

**Reason:** A drawing on cream or black paper should preview as it will plot. Inks are transparent, so on coloured paper the paper's colour multiplies with the ink's, which the multiply compositing of "Frames per pen" already models. Kept out of `Canvas`, the paper enters no stage's cache key, so changing it repaints the preview without rerunning a stage. Multiplying the outline too makes the paper's effect one rule: a picture on paper `P` is the white-paper picture times `P`, channel by channel. The property suite checks the part of that rule that 8-bit blending keeps exactly, in the law "the paper shows where nothing is drawn and ink only darkens it". Every pixel that is white on white paper is exactly `P`, and no channel is above both `P` and the white-paper pixel by more than one level. The exact product is not the law because tiny-skia rounds at each blend. In dense drawings on dark paper the rounding added up to 27 to 35 levels, though single strokes stay within 2 or 3. An optional key keeps every saved preset and every embedded export loading unchanged. SVG and G-code go to a plotter, which draws only the paths, and a background rectangle in the SVG could be drawn by mistake. Opaque inks, such as white gel on black paper, cannot be shown by multiplying and preview as nothing. White ink on dark paper also needs the tone inverted, and choosing inks for a coloured paper needs the colour model to start from that paper. Both are larger changes, left until someone asks for them.
