# Plot playback

**Decided:** 2026-10-17

**Choice:** The GUI preview gets a Play chip in the card at its top left, beside Compare, and turning one on turns the other off. Play shows a bar along the bottom with play and pause, a time slider from 0 to the plot time, and the time gone and the time left as m:ss, or h:mm:ss from an hour. The preview draws the finished drawing as far as the pen has got: each layer's whole paths in plot order, pen by pen, and the path being drawn up to the pen. The pen-up move under way shows faint and dashed from the lift to the landing, and the pen shows as a ring. The whole plot plays in 30 seconds of the page's clock, and Play from the end starts again. The slider counts in thousandths of the plot and handles its own keys: the arrows move a hundredth of the plot, Page Up and Page Down a tenth, and Home and End go to the ends. A range input snaps its value to its step, so a native key that would move it within one step did nothing.

The times come from the engine. `stats::timeline(drawing, speeds)` walks the drawing as `stats::compute` times it, with the same trapezoidal moves and corner speeds (see [acceleration in the plot time](2026-10-17-acceleration-in-the-plot-time.md)), and returns where the pen starts and, for each layer, when each path's pen-up move starts and ends and when the pen reaches each vertex. Each path's `pen_lift_s` is split: half lifting where the pen is, then the move, then half lowering onto the path. `Session::timeline` gives it for the last drawing at the session's speeds, the wasm `Session.timeline()` hands it over a layer at a time as typed arrays, and the protocol's `timeline` command returns it. The page asks again when the result or the stats change, so a change of speeds re-times the playback. It ignores a timeline that does not match the drawing on screen, with one time per vertex and one move per path. The page only finds where a time falls among the engine's times and puts the pen on a straight line between the two points it falls between. The law `the_timeline_rises_and_ends_at_the_plot_time` in `crates/core/tests/laws.rs` checks that the times never go back and that the last one is the estimated plot time. Vitest covers the scrub mapping and the slider's keys, and a browser flow covers Play, the keys, and the hand-over with Compare.

**Reason:** The plot time is a single number. Playback shows where it goes: which parts plot slowly, how much the pen travels, and the order the pen will take, which the travel overlay shows only all at once. Taking the times from the engine keeps the playback and the stats from disagreeing, and keeps the motion model in the core, as the brief requires. Times for every vertex, rather than for each path alone, let a single long path, such as a spiral or a single-line drawing, grow during its plot instead of appearing at its end. They cost 8 bytes a vertex, and the page asks for them only while Play is on. A fixed 30-second playback suits plots from minutes to hours without a speed setting, and the slider reaches any moment.
