# Auto framing

**Decided:** 2026-10-16

**Choice:** Added on 2026-10-16 under [the auto framing spec](../superpowers/specs/2026-10-16-auto-framing-design.md). It amends the "Canvas modes" row of [the brief's decision log](../BRIEF.md#14-decision-log), which planned a crop as `fill` plus an offset, and adds a rule to the "Suggestion cut-offs" row.

- **The crop is on the canvas.** `Canvas.crop` holds the shares of the source image cut from its left, top, right and bottom, each 0 to 0.45 (`Canvas::with_crop` checks them and names the bad one; `Canvas::new` is unchanged). While all four are zero the field is left out of the canvas's JSON, so presets, exports and the golden files keep their bytes. The tone stage and the colour split in Pen use cut the crop from the image first, each edge rounded to whole source pixels (halves to even), then for `fill` cut the middle to the drawing area's aspect, then resize. One function, `tone::source_rect`, gives both stages that rectangle, and `tone::map_size`, which the tone-response wedge uses, reads it too. The `orientation` suggestion counts only the part of the image that is drawn, so a crop to the page's shape does not ask for a turn. `plotline run` and `analyse` take `--crop LEFT,TOP,RIGHT,BOTTOM`, MCP `set_page` takes `crop`, Python's `Canvas` has `crop`, and the GUI's Page panel shows Cropped with a Remove crop button. The crop itself comes only from the suggestion; Undo takes it back like any page change.
- **Saliency** is the spectral residual of X. Hou and L. Zhang, "Saliency detection: a spectral residual approach", *Proc. CVPR*, 2007, pp. 1-8, doi:10.1109/CVPR.2007.383267, written from the paper: the luminance block-averaged to 64 pixels on its longer side, a 2D transform, `R = ln|F| - mean3x3(ln|F|)`, and `|F^-1[exp(R + iP)]|^2` smoothed with a Gaussian of 8 pixels. It differs from the paper in the boundary. A Fourier transform treats the map as one period of an endless tiling, so the jump between its opposite edges reads as salient; on the generated scene the first version put the saliency in the corners and along the right edge, not on the sun or the tower. The map is therefore mirrored about its edges before the transform, which is the boundary the discrete cosine transform implies (G. Strang, "The discrete cosine transform", *SIAM Review* 41(1), 1999, pp. 135-147, doi:10.1137/S0036144598336745). The mirror is about the edge pixels' own rows and columns, so a `w` by `h` map becomes `2 (w - 1)` by `2 (h - 1)`. That is Strang's type 1, whose spectrum is real and whose phase is the sign of each coefficient. The mirror the first attempt used, across the pixels' outer edges (type 2, `2 w` by `2 h`), makes the transform zero all along its highest frequency row and column; the residual then blows up their neighbours, and the map showed one blob in the middle of every picture, a disc's or noise alike. The transform is written out as a direct separable sum of cosines from `math::cos`, equal to the Fourier transform of the mirrored map and checked against that, summed term by term, in a test. There is no FFT crate. The saliency step takes about 1.3 ms in a native release build on a 256 by 256 picture (the whole of `measure` takes 4.6 ms there). The smoothing is of the map mirrored out to its period, and the top-left `w` by `h` is kept. `analysis::measure` makes the map once and keeps it out of the statistics' JSON. An image under 8 pixels on a side, or whose 64-pixel map has tones differing by under 1/64, gets no map: a flat image's transform is rounding noise, and its residual would show it as a subject.
- **The crop search** follows R. Suh, H. Ling, B. B. Bederson and D. W. Jacobs, "Automatic thumbnail cropping and its effectiveness", *Proc. UIST*, 2003, pp. 95-104, doi:10.1145/964696.964707: the smallest rectangle holding a share `lambda` of the saliency, with `lambda` taken before the largest rise in the area needed. Two changes for plotline. The rectangle has the drawing area's aspect, so the cropped image fills the drawing area in either fit mode. On a 64-pixel map the search is exhaustive over a summed-area table (15 sizes from 1 down to 0.3 in steps of 0.05, every pixel position, `lambda` from 0.50 to 0.95 by 0.05) instead of greedy. Where no size holds a `lambda`, its area counts as 1. The crop shares are rounded down to 0.01, so the subject is never cut, and kept to 0.45.
- **Cut-offs.** A crop is suggested only if its area is at most 0.7 of the largest rectangle of the page's shape that fits the image, and its saliency per unit area is at least 1.3 times the whole image's. These are the spec's starting values and stay as they were after trying them on the generated images (the table below). A tolerance of 0.01 per side decides that the canvas already has the suggested crop.

**Reason:** Two stages read where the image sits, the tone stage for the luminance and the colour split for its colour planes, and neither can read the other's settings, so the crop is a property of the page they both read; a stage setting would have meant setting it twice. Leaving the field out while it is zero is the only way that keeps the bytes of every existing preset and export. The generated test images separate on the two cut-offs: the discs, the only images with a subject, pass both with room when the page lets the crop be a good deal smaller than the image (area at most 0.64 against 0.7, density 1.37 and more against 1.3), while every image without a subject has an area of 0.81 or more, and the noise, gradient and texture images also have a density near 1, which is no concentration at all. The still life and the scene fill their frames, so having no suggestion is the right answer for them; the scene's map now peaks on the tower and the sun, and the cut-off, not the map, is what keeps it quiet. A disc whose crop would need 0.72 of the largest rectangle of the page's shape (the third disc on A4 landscape) gets no suggestion: a portrait or landscape rectangle cut from a square picture already drops a third of one side, so there is little room for a further cut, and the cut-off stays at the spec's value rather than moving for the edge of a handful of cases. The 0.45 cap on a side leaves at least a tenth of the image, and the downward rounding of each share keeps a crop from cutting into the subject. No FFT crate is used because the common one picks its kernels at run time, and the native and browser builds must agree to the last bit.
