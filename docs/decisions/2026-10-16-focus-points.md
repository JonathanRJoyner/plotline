# Focus points

**Decided:** 2026-10-16

**Choice:** Added on 2026-10-16 under [the focus points spec](../superpowers/specs/2026-10-16-focus-points-design.md), after D. DeCarlo and A. Santella, "Stylization and abstraction of photographs", *ACM Transactions on Graphics* 21(3), 2002, pp. 769-776, doi:10.1145/566654.566650. Three settings join the shared `tone` stage: `focus`, a list of at most 16 points as `[x, y]` shares of the source image's width and height from its top-left corner (default empty); `focus_mm`, the radius around each point that keeps all its detail (1 to 300 mm, default 40); and `focus_blur_mm`, the blur furthest from every point (0.5 to 10 mm, default 3). After the existing global blur, and only with a point, a pixel at distance `d` mm from the nearest point takes the weight `w = clamp((d - focus_mm) / (2 focus_mm), 0, 1)`: the map is blurred with `math::blur` at a quarter, a half, three quarters and all of `focus_blur_mm`, and the pixel takes its ink linearly between the two levels either side of `4 w`, level 0 being the unblurred map. A pixel within `focus_mm` keeps its ink exactly, and with no points the stage's output is the same bit for bit, so no golden file, gallery cell or snapshot line moved. A point is placed through `Canvas::page_point`, and the GUI finds the point under a click through `Canvas::image_point`; both go through one private `Canvas::shown`, the part of the source the page shows (all of it for fit, the `fill` crop for fill, from `tone::crop_rect`, which `crop_to_aspect` also uses), so a canvas crop extends one place. A point outside the part shown lies off the map and still counts. `Session::image_point` and `Session::page_point` give both for the current image, and the wasm `Session` passes them through; the Python `Session` does not, since nothing there needs them. The stage refuses more than 16 points and a point that is not two numbers from 0 to 1, with a message naming the point (`stage 'tone', setting 'focus': point 2 is [1.5, 0.2]; each point must be two numbers from 0 to 1`); the setting is a `Vec<Vec<f64>>` rather than `Vec<[f64; 2]>` for that, since serde's own message for a point of one number names neither the setting nor the point. The schema still says what the list holds: `maxItems` 16 and items of exactly two numbers from 0 to 1, which the property laws read to draw 0 to 3 random points. `"x-hidden": true` is a new schema extension: the GUI's form leaves the key out (`controlsFor` in `web/src/schema.ts`) and sets it through the preview, while the CLI, MCP and library see it like any other. `--set tone.focus=[[0.5,0.3]]` works because `coerce` reads text that starts with `[` or `{` as JSON, and the MCP `run` docstring tells an assistant that it can set the points where it sees a face or a subject. In the GUI a Focus chip in the toolbar, in the Fit page group because a group of its own made the phone's two-row toolbar three rows, turns on focus mode: a click on the drawn image adds a point, a click within 3 mm (at least 8 px on screen) of one removes it, and a drag still pans. The rings are an SVG layer over the canvas, not painted on it, so the stylesheet colours them and each removal target is a real button the browser flow can click. The paper is white in both themes, so a ring is a dark indigo (`#4338ca`, 8.6 to 1 on white) in both, with a white casing under it for where it runs past the paper onto the dark backdrop; the first version used the theme's accent, which is pale in the dark theme and faded on the paper. Edits are chained through the list the page holds: each sends that list changed and the page holds the new one at once, so two clicks before the first reply both land, where building on the last reply lost all but the last, and a point is removed by its value, since an edit in between moves its place in the list. Their page positions come with every reply as `State.focus`, worked out by the worker with `pagePoint`, so a ring never lags the settings. Every change is one `update` of `tone.focus`, which reruns from the tone stage, joins the undo history and saves with the setup; the Image section shows "N focus points" and a Clear button above `focus_mm` (labelled Focus radius) and `focus_blur_mm`, which stay plain settings. With several pens the falloff applies to the tone map that Pen use shares out, so every lane is abstracted alike. By colour, a lane's chroma comes from the image, so only the luminance part of each lane is blurred. The wedge that auto Response measures uses the plain tone defaults, so focus does not change the lookup. `settings_matter.rs` lists `focus_mm` and `focus_blur_mm` of every style as no-ops "used only with focus points, which are off by default", and `truchet.tone.focus`, since on its 64 by 48 noise image no tile's arc count crosses a rounding point (on the still life it draws differently). A law in `laws.rs`, `focus_points_keep_the_ink_within_their_radius`, runs 12 of its cases per pull request over every style and finds each tone-map pixel within `focus_mm` of a point with the ink it has without focus. The tone map of the focused still life is pinned natively in `session.rs` and in the WebAssembly smoke test.

**Reason:** The spec compared the routes. DeCarlo and Santella remove detail from regions away from where a viewer looked, by a contrast-sensitivity model over a region hierarchy; a blur whose strength grows with distance from the nearest point is the same falloff in a form that runs on the tone map every style already reads, so no style needed code. Weighting the tone by importance, or the placers' point density, changes how dark a region draws rather than how much detail it keeps, and a weight per placer would be per-style code. A blur keeps the local mean of the ink, so a region away from the focus draws about as dark as before with its fine structure gone, and a style's density setting then spends the same marks on smoother tone. On the still life the mean ink moved by under 2 % in the unit test. A saliency detector that places the points was left out: auto framing uses saliency for a crop, and for focus the choice of the person or the assistant is the point. Levels at a quarter step of the blur and a linear blend keep the cost to four blurs and one pass; a median of seven release runs on Windows of the tone stage on the still life took 4.8 ms without points and 65 to 71 ms with one or three at 1000 px, and 24 ms and about 0.6 s at 2500 px, which only `ink`, whose default is that resolution, or a user who raises it, meets. The chip's width moved the thresholds of [the narrow toolbar](2026-10-16-narrow-toolbar.md), which that decision's amendment records. The page positions travel in the state because the alternative, asking the worker for each ring after each change, leaves a ring behind the settings for a frame and adds a call per point. Placing points through the source image's size rather than the tone map's leaves them off by under a pixel of the map, where the rounding of the map's size differs from the image's aspect. Built on Windows, the WebAssembly module was 2,045,478 bytes before and 2,065,405 bytes after, 19,927 bytes more, under the 2,300,000 the CI checks.
