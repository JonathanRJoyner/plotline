# Test chart

**Decided:** 2026-10-16

**Choice:** Built on 2026-10-16 under [the test chart spec](../superpowers/specs/2026-10-16-testchart-design.md). `Session::testchart` makes one layer for the first pen on the session's page and claims the result slot as a run does, so the stats, lint, preview and exports work on it and no image is needed. `plotline testchart`, the MCP `testchart` tool (the fourteenth), `Session.testchart()` and the GUI's Test chart button call it. Every size follows the pen width `w`: a patch is `P = max(10, 8 w)` mm square with `P / 2` between patches and rows, text `T = max(2.5, 5 w)` mm high, and each label sits `T / 2` under its patch. Three sections follow a header that gives the pen. `SPACING MM` has horizontal lines `f w` apart for f of 0.5, 0.75, 1, 1.25, 1.5, 2, 3 and 4. `DOT MM` has grids of dots `f w` wide for f of 1, 1.5, 2, 3, 4 and 6, at pitch `max(2 d, d + w)`. `PASSES` has the solid fill (lines one pen width apart) drawn 1, 2, 3, 4, 6 and 8 times. The labels use a single-stroke font of original polylines on a 4 by 6 grid, in `text.rs`; no font file or third-party glyph data is used. Three additions to the spec: a pen under 0.05 mm is refused, because the dot grids would hold millions of dots; a page too narrow is refused with the width in place of the height; and when a label is wider than the space between patches, which happens above about a 0.7 mm pen, the patches move apart by the label's width plus one text height, so labels never touch. On A4 with a 0.3 mm pen the chart is three rows under a header, 70 mm of the 277 mm of drawing area, with 1,669 paths, 11.2 m of pen-down line and about 9.7 minutes at the default speeds; with a 1 mm pen it is 513 paths. With main of 2026-10-16 (1b571e5) merged, the WebAssembly module is 1,913,538 bytes

**Reason:** The engine's physical numbers are guesses until someone checks them on paper: the lint calls three passes overdraw, the plot time assumes the speeds given, and a dot is assumed to ink its drawn diameter. Spacings from half a pen width to four bracket the point where lines merge, with fine steps around one pen width, where the lint's `close_lines` starts to warn. A dot of one pen width is a point, the smallest the engine draws, and six is a spiral of several turns. The passes bracket the lint's three-pass threshold and go on to eight, well past it. A patch of 10 mm holds a 2 by 2 grid of the widest dots at 0.3 mm and reads at arm's length; 8 w keeps a patch at least 16 of the finest lines tall for thick pens. Text of 5 w makes a grid unit 0.83 w, so most gaps inside a glyph are wider than the pen, and 2.5 mm is the least a 0.3 mm pen still writes legibly in the preview. A pitch of twice the diameter leaves a full dot between neighbours, so each dot's size can be measured. The chart reads, by eye, what the user then sets by hand; nothing feeds back automatically, and a chart for every pen at once or a speed ladder is left for later
