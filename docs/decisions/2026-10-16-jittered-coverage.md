# Plot lint samples each raster cell at a point of its own

**Decided:** 2026-10-16. Amends the brief's "Coverage raster" row.

**Choice:** The plot lint's measure stamps the drawing into a jittered coverage raster, `Coverage::jittered`. Each cell is sampled at one point inside it instead of at its centre, which is jittered sampling (R. L. Cook, "Stochastic sampling in computer graphics", *ACM Trans. Graphics* 5(1), 1986, pp. 51-72). The point is placed by the SplitMix64 output function (G. L. Steele Jr., D. Lea and C. H. Flood, "Fast splittable pseudorandom number generators", *OOPSLA* 2014) of the cell's column and row, so it needs no other cell's and is the same on every target. A row of a segment is now a band one cell high: the cells inside the capsule all the way across the band count without a test, and only those its edge crosses test their point. One raster still serves every count, so the tone match and the `dark` and `overdraw` findings all read the jittered raster. `Coverage::new` keeps centre sampling, with the same counts as before, and the tone response wedge keeps using it with its widened cells.

**Reason:** A hatch line at exactly 45° keeps one phase against the 0.1 mm cell centres along its whole length, so a 0.2 mm pen inks either 2 or 3 diagonal rows of cells. When the spacing times √2 is near a whole number of cells, every line of a level snaps the same way, and the tone error jumps between neighbouring spacings. The same holds at 0° and 90° for pens whose half-width is not a whole number of cells. This was the open follow-up of [the Optimize decision](2026-10-16-optimize-limits.md): on a user's pale line art it picked 0.583 mm at 47 min over 0.825 mm at 30 min for a gain the size of this jump.

Measured natively with `hatch` at 3 levels and threshold 0.12, a 0.2 mm pen and a 200 mm square page, over 17 spacings from 0.53 to 0.61 mm, as the spread of the tone error (largest minus smallest, over the mean):

| Image | 45° before | 44.7° before | 45° after | 44.7° after | 45° on 0.025 mm cells | 44.7° on 0.025 mm cells |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| pale line art (`strokes` 0.6 on 1) | 172 % | 111 % | 9.2 % | 9.3 % | | |
| line art lit from one side | 13.1 % | 7.0 % | 8.5 % | 7.3 % | 8.9 % | 7.1 % |
| still life | 9.0 % | 6.0 % | 7.3 % | 6.3 % | 7.3 % | 6.3 % |

At 45° the jittered raster now matches a centre-sampled raster four times finer, so what is left between 45° and 44.7° is where hatch's lines fall on the image, not the raster. At 0° against 0.3° the spreads went from 10.7 and 9.4 % to 8.9 and 10.0 % on the lit line art, and from 11.5 and 9.5 % to 11.2 and 9.4 % on the still life. On the gradient every angle spreads by 19 to 28 % before and after, from where the levels' thresholds cut the lines. The test `hatch_at_45_degrees_scores_as_steadily_as_off_the_raster` scans a 100 mm page in 13 steps of 0.0025 mm: 131 % at 45° against 92 % at 44.7° before, 11 % against 15 % after.

On single lines and sets of lines, the raster's error became noise. Over 10,000 random cases, half at 0, 45, 90 or 135°, with pens from 0.1 to 1 mm, a single 20 mm line's inked area was off by up to 84 % with centre sampling (99th percentile 34 %) and by up to 11 % jittered (99th percentile 5.8 %, the thinnest pens, whose every cell is an edge cell). Eight parallel lines 2 mm apart, which centre sampling snaps alike, were off by up to 84 % (99th percentile 30 %) and by up to 4.0 % jittered (99th percentile 2.2 %). The law `a_jittered_raster_covers_lines_at_their_width_at_any_angle_and_place` holds the eight lines within 6 %.

Two offset rules were tried first and dropped. Offsets per column and per row, stepping by the golden and silver ratios, fixed 45° but kept one offset along a horizontal line, so each line's width became a random 1 to 3 rows: the 0° spread on the still life rose from 11.5 to 23.1 %. Offsets per cell stepping by the fractional parts of √2, √3, √5 and √7 locked to lines near 33.7°, where 3√5 + 2√7 is within 0.0003 of 12, and missed a thin line's area by 37 %. A hash has no direction of its own for a line to lock to.

A second, centre-sampled raster for the other counts would have doubled the stamping. Jittered sampling is no less exact for them, only unbiased where centres were biased: the pinned `dark` value of a 2 mm grid of 0.3 mm dots moved from 0.0150 to 0.0178, against the 0.0177 that the dots' area over the grid's gives, because every dot sat at the same phase on the cells. The pinned `waves` tone match moved from 0.0840 to 0.0820, its SSIM from 0.731 to 0.749, and with the auto response from 0.0907 to 0.0888. In the regression gallery no drawing and no lint finding changed; the tone error moved in 85 of the 126 cells, by 0.0025 at the median (2.9 %). The largest moves were `contours` on the gradient (2.84 to 2.70 at 0.5 mm, 2.14 to 2.05 at 0.1 mm, a style the tone match cannot follow), `line` on the scene at 0.1 mm (0.167 to 0.179) and `hilbert` on the gradient at 0.1 mm (0.148 to 0.137).

The edge test costs time where most cells are edge cells. Natively on the still life at A4 with the defaults, stamping took 1.3 to 1.8 times as long as centred: `hatch` 15 to 19 ms, `waves` 82 to 141 ms with a 0.3 mm pen, `stipple` 84 to 151 ms with a 0.1 mm pen, `scribble` 73 to 108 ms, and a few ms more for `grid`, `dither` and `flow`. The whole measure, with the tone match's blurs, takes 42 to 242 ms, so an Optimize trial costs 4 to 67 ms more. The WebAssembly module grew by 2,878 bytes, from 2,128,198 to 2,131,076, under the 2,300,000-byte limit.
