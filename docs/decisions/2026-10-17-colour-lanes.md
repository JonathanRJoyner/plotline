# Colour lanes: gamma after the colour solve, lifts below half a bare line, points shared by ink

**Decided:** 2026-10-17. Amends the brief's "Colour model" and "Empty lanes" rows. Settles findings F4, F5 and F6 of [the colour and styles audit](../audits/2026-10-16-colour-styles-audit.md).

**Choice:**

1. **Gamma after the solve.** In `by_colour` mode the split stage moves each colour to the ink the tone map had before its gamma, `x = 1 - (1 - t)^(1 / gamma)`, solves the pens' coverages there, and puts each coverage `a` through the tone stage's curve, `1 - (1 - a)^gamma`. The tone map carries the gamma it was made with (`ToneMap::gamma`, 1 where no gamma applies). Every other Image setting still carries over by the luminance rule. A gamma of 1 gives the maps bit for bit as before.
2. **Lanes lift below half a bare line.** When the pens share a drawing, `waves`, `spiral` and `hilbert` lift a pen where its lane's ink is under half of what the style's lines cover over white, `pen width / spacing` with the spacing of `waves` and `spiral` and the `max_cell_mm` of `hilbert`, and never under the old 0.02. These stages now read the lane's pen. With one pen they draw whole lines as before. `flow` keeps the old 0.02.
3. **Points per pen, shared by ink by colour.** `voronoi.count` stays a budget per pen, so a drawing with `n` pens places `n * count` points as before. By colour, the pens share those `n` budgets in proportion to each pen's total ink instead of each spending one. Light to dark and one pen keep one whole budget per pen. The split stage decides: its output, `Tones`, carries each map's part of the budgets, `[start, end]` counted in whole budgets. By colour the parts are running totals of the maps' ink scaled to `n`, so they meet and end at exactly `n`; otherwise each is `[0, 1]`. The runner hands each lane its part (`RunContext::budget`, in each lane stage's cache key), and the placer takes `round(count * end) - round(count * start)` points.

**Reason:** Measured with `crates/core/examples/colour_lanes.rs`, which draws each style on two test images, all with 0.3 mm pens on A4. The first is a poster drawn in code: a red disc, a yellow square, a black bar and half tints of the red and yellow, on white. The second is `synthetic::scene`. Both are drawn by colour with Yellow `#f5d000`, Red `#d0202e` and Black pens, and the scene also light to dark with Grey and Black. On the poster it measures the line each pen draws outside the areas of its own colour. For every drawing it takes the colour measure of the audit (`common/colour.rs`, shared with `audit.rs`): the drawing and the image are blurred by the tone match's 1.5 mm eye filter and compared in CIE Lab. The gain is how much nearer the drawing is to the image than blank paper. The tone error measures luminance only, so it cannot see a pen drawing the wrong hue. Before is `main` at dd5c5bd, with its acceleration-aware plot time.

No case at its defaults loses gain. The poster's black pen in `waves` fell from 18.6 m of line to 5.1 m. The one change light to dark makes at its defaults is `spiral`'s second pen lifting at its band's lightest edge: its ΔE went from 50.17 to 50.18, a gain of 0.294 both times.

The gamma after the solve did most of the work for the rippling styles. With it alone the poster's `waves` drew 7.7 m of black, as it does at a gamma of 1 (7.6 m), and gained 0.623. That confirms the audit's cause: the gamma had been adding grey. Applying the gamma to each pen's share keeps what the rippling gamma is for (lines ink in proportion to the tone, so the midtones need more), while a red stays a red. The curve and its inverse cancel, so one black pen on a grey image still gets the ink `one_pen` gives it. Under a gamma of about 0.5 the curve is steep enough near full ink that the solve's small ink penalty shows. A black pen then draws pure black at 0.987 of full ink at a gamma of 0.5, and at 0.58 at the least gamma, 0.1, which is the ink one pen gives a lightness of 2e-4, under one step of an 8-bit image. The gamma could instead have been set to 1 by colour, as the audit's stop-gap suggested, but that loses the midtones the gamma was set for.

For the lift, half a bare line beat the whole line the audit proposed. A bare line covers its share of the paper however little ink is below it. Between none and that share, paper and line are the two levels a pen can give, and below half the share paper is the nearer one, as a two-level quantiser picks the level nearest its input. On the poster both thresholds removed the off-colour line of `waves` (0.6 and 0.3 m left) at the same gain, 0.652. But lifting below a whole line lowered the gain of `flow` from 0.278 to 0.265, of `hilbert` from 0.546 to 0.534, and of `waves` on the scene light to dark at a gamma of 1 from 0.241 to 0.226. Half a line left `hilbert` unchanged and that `waves` at 0.229. At its defaults `hilbert` (8 mm folds) lifts at the old 0.02, so it changes only with smaller folds. Each leaf's own size was tried first for `hilbert`, but in its strips one cell wide, which the curve walks cell by cell even in light areas, that lifted the pen over ink of 0.15 and left gaps. The spacing over white is the line the rule is about.

`flow` keeps the old 0.02. With half a line (0.025 at its defaults) the poster's black pen stopped drawing 0.8 m over the pink square, but the gain fell from 0.278 to 0.271. `flow` covers far less paper than its ink asks everywhere but near white, so even a stray pen's line had been darkening a square it draws too light. No default drawing may get lighter, since drawings already come out too light, so that change waits until `flow` inks in proportion. The same holds for two cases off the defaults. At a gamma of 1 the poster's `waves` lost gain, 0.602 to 0.586, and `waves` on the scene light to dark lost 0.241 to 0.229. In both, the stray yellow and black it no longer draws had darkened colours that `waves` at that gamma draws too light.

For the points, the audit's finding stands: every pen placed all of `count`. On the scene the red barn, a sliver of the picture, crowded 4995 dots into a solid block, and the poster's thin black bar got as many dots as the red disc and the pink square together. Shared by ink at the same total, the drawings came nearer to the images in every by-colour case measured, as the table shows. With 15,000 points per pen the poster's `stipple` rose from 0.852 to 0.918 and the scene's from 0.234 to 0.294. Light to dark, sharing by ink made the drawings worse at the same total: `stipple` on the scene went from 0.047 to 0.037, `line` from 0.092 to 0.075, and `scribble` held at 0.237. There each later pen draws only its band's darkest tones, and a whole budget of its own darkens the shadows of styles that draw too light at their defaults (finding F7). So only by colour shares.

The total was first set to `count` itself, as the audit proposed. At the defaults that made a three-pen drawing place a third of the points it did, and the poster's `stipple` gain fell from 0.396 to 0.132, so the total stays `count` per pen. Two smaller totals were measured or worked out and not chosen. The first gave each pen the points one pen would place over the same amount of tone, `count` times its ink over the tone map's. It drew fewer points (9,535 on the poster, 6,780 on the scene) and needs the tone map before the split in every lane. The second was `count` times the effective number of pens, one over the sum of their squared shares of the ink, which would hold back the budgets of pens with almost no ink; by the scene's shares it would place about 9,400 points instead of 15,000. With `count` per pen, a pen whose colour the picture barely has still adds its budget, which by colour now goes to the pens that draw: four pens on a picture that only one of them can draw put about four budgets into that one. Having the placer read every pen's map was also tried, but a stage that requires the split's maps cannot run in a style without a split, which a style may be.

In the regression gallery, which draws with one pen, no cell changed.
