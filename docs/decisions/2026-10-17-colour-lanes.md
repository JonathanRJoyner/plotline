# Colour lanes: gamma after the colour solve, lifts below half a bare line, points shared by ink

**Decided:** 2026-10-17. Amends the brief's "Colour model" and "Empty lanes" rows. Settles findings F4, F5 and F6 of [the colour and styles audit](../audits/2026-10-16-colour-styles-audit.md).

**Choice:**

1. **Gamma after the solve.** In `by_colour` mode the split stage moves each colour to the ink the tone map had before its gamma, `x = 1 - (1 - t)^(1 / gamma)`, solves the pens' coverages there, and puts each coverage `a` through the tone stage's curve, `1 - (1 - a)^gamma`. The tone map carries the gamma it was made with (`ToneMap::gamma`, 1 where no gamma applies). Every other Image setting still carries over by the luminance rule. A gamma of 1 gives the maps bit for bit as before.
2. **Lanes lift below half a bare line.** When the pens share a drawing, `waves`, `spiral`, `flow` and `hilbert` lift a pen where its lane's ink is under half of what the style's lines cover over white, `pen width / spacing` with the spacing of `waves` and `spiral`, the `max_spacing_mm` of `flow` and the `max_cell_mm` of `hilbert`, and never under the old 0.02. These stages now read the lane's pen. With one pen they draw whole lines as before.
3. **Points shared by ink.** When the pens share a drawing, the Voronoi placer gives each lane its share of `count` in proportion to the lane's total ink. The runner, which opens the lanes, gives each stage after the split its lane's part of the run's ink as running totals, `[start, end]` of 0 to 1 (`RunContext::ink`), and the placer takes `round(count * end) - round(count * start)` points, so the lanes place `count` exactly and a lane with no ink places none. With one pen the part is `[0, 1]` and nothing changes. The part is in each lane stage's cache key. Reading every pen's map in the placer itself was tried first, but a stage that requires the split's maps cannot run in a style without a split, which a style may be.

**Reason:** Measured with `crates/core/examples/colour_lanes.rs`, which draws each style by colour on a poster drawn in code (a red disc, a yellow square, a black bar and half tints of the red and yellow, on white) with Yellow `#f5d000`, Red `#d0202e` and Black pens, on `synthetic::scene` with the same pens, and on the scene light to dark with Grey and Black, all 0.3 mm on A4. Besides each layer's length it measures, on the poster, the line each pen draws outside the areas of its own colour, and for every drawing the colour measure of the audit: the drawing and the image blurred by the tone match's 1.5 mm eye filter and compared in CIE Lab (`common/colour.rs`, shared with `audit.rs`), where the gain is how much nearer the drawing is to the image than blank paper. The tone error measures luminance only, so it cannot see a pen drawing the wrong hue. Before is `main` at e0affff.

On the scene the gain of `waves` went from 0.415 to 0.427 and of `spiral` from 0.418 to 0.434; `mosaic` held at 0.406 to 0.407. At a gamma of 1 the poster's `waves` drew 7.6 m of black, as with the gamma after the solve, which confirms the audit's cause: the gamma had been adding grey. Applying the gamma to each pen's share keeps what the rippling gamma is for (lines ink in proportion to the tone, so the midtones need more), while a red stays a red.

The curve and its inverse cancel, so one black pen on a grey image still gets the ink `one_pen` gives it. Under a gamma of about 0.5 the curve is steep enough near full ink that the solve's small ink penalty shows: a black pen draws pure black at 0.987 of full ink at a gamma of 0.5, and at 0.58 at the least gamma, 0.1, which is the ink one pen gives a lightness of 2e-4, under one step of an 8-bit image. The gamma could instead have been set to 1 by colour, as the audit's stop-gap suggested, but that loses the midtones the gamma was set for.

For the lift, half a bare line beat the whole line the audit proposed. A bare line covers its share of the paper however little ink is below it, so between none and that share, paper and line are the two levels a pen can give, and the nearer one wins under half, as a two-level quantiser picks the level nearest its input. On the poster both thresholds removed the off-colour line of `waves` (0.6 and 0.3 m left) at the same gain, 0.652, but lifting below a whole line lowered the gain of `flow` from 0.278 to 0.265 and of `hilbert` from 0.546 to 0.534 and of `waves` on the scene light to dark at a gamma of 1 from 0.241 to 0.226, where half a line left `flow` at 0.271, `hilbert` unchanged and that `waves` at 0.229. At their defaults `flow` (6 mm over white) and `hilbert` (8 mm folds) lift at their old 0.02 or near it, so they change little. Two costs: at a gamma of 1 the poster's `waves` lost gain, 0.602 to 0.586, because the stray yellow and black it no longer draws had darkened colours that `waves` at that gamma draws too light; and `waves` on the scene light to dark at a gamma of 1 lost the same way, 0.241 to 0.229, where the black pen no longer draws its band's lightest edge. Each leaf's own size was tried first for `hilbert`: in its strips one cell wide, which the curve walks cell by cell even in light areas, that lifted the pen over ink of 0.15 and left gaps. The spacing over white is the line the rule is about, and in `flow` the spacing at a point's own ink is within a few percent of it at inks near the floor, so every style takes its spacing over white.

For the points, the audit's finding stands: before, every pen placed all of `count`, so on the scene the red barn, a sliver of the picture, crowded 4995 dots into a solid block, and the poster's thin black bar got as many dots as the red disc and the pink square together. Shared by ink at the same total, the drawings came nearer to the images by colour in every case measured: at 15,000 points in all (three pens of 5,000 before, a `count` of 15,000 after), the poster's gain went from 0.396 to 0.405 for `stipple`, 0.304 to 0.315 for `line` and 0.814 to 0.842 for `scribble`, and the scene's from 0.103 to 0.107, 0.158 to 0.172 and 0.348 to 0.380. Light to dark on the scene, at 10,000 points in all, they went from 0.047 to 0.037, 0.092 to 0.075 and 0.237 to 0.237: there the black pen's equal share had darkened the shadows of styles that draw too light at their defaults (finding F7), and its share now follows its band's ink.

The total is `count`, as the audit proposed, so `count` means the points of the whole drawing whatever the pens, a dot budget in Fit asks for about that many points, and a drawing with three pens plots in about the time one pen does. At the default 5,000 points a three-pen drawing therefore places a third of the points it did and comes out lighter: the poster's `stipple` gain fell from 0.396 to 0.132 and its plot time from 64.5 to 22.6 min. Two other totals were measured and not chosen. Keeping the old total, `count` times the pens, kept the darkness, but an unused pen, such as cyan, magenta and yellow on a grey picture, would hand its share to black and draw it darker than one pen. Giving each pen the points one pen would place over the same amount of tone, `count` times its ink over the tone map's, drew 9,535 points on the poster and 6,780 on the scene, but needs the tone map before the split in every lane and makes `count` mean nothing a person sees. How many points a picture needs is the subject of F7, which is a separate change; until then a by-colour drawing that should be darker needs a higher count, which the Faithful and Fine looks and Fit already set.

In the regression gallery, which draws with one pen, no cell changed.
