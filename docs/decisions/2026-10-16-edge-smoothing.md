# Edge-keeping smoothing

**Decided:** 2026-10-16

**Choice:** Built under [the edge smoothing spec](../superpowers/specs/2026-10-16-edge-smoothing-design.md). The tone stage has a setting `smooth_mm`, labelled "Smooth, keep edges", under Advanced beside Blur, from 0 to 10 mm, defaulting to 0, which is off. When set, the luminance is smoothed after flattening and before the levels by the guided filter of He, Sun and Tang (ECCV 2010, TPAMI 2013), with the image as its own guide, a window of `2r + 1` pixels for `r = smooth_mm / mm_per_px`, and a fixed penalty `eps = 0.1^2`. Its four box means are running sums (Wells 1986) through the new `math::box_mean`, and the tone run takes four steps for them, labelled `smooth`, so Stop can act between them. With it off the tone map and the run's frames are the same as before. A law says the smoothed luminance stays within the image's own tone range. The "Clean up a scan" tour step gains a sentence and a browser flow sets it on `contours`.

**Reason:** Blur softens edges as much as noise, and contours, flow, ink and hatch's level boundaries follow edges. On a 0.2 to 0.8 step with noise of standard deviation 0.03 and a 4-pixel radius, the guided filter keeps a jump of 0.53 across the step where a Gaussian of the same sigma leaves 0.06, and it lowers the noise 7.7 times where the Gaussian lowers it 10 times. A direct bilateral filter costs the square of the radius per pixel, minutes at a 4000-pixel map, and its fast approximations are a larger job; the guided filter costs the same at any radius and reuses the crate's running-sum box filter. At a 4000 by 2800 map the filter takes about 0.53 s in a release build, in four steps of 108 to 141 ms. The penalty is fixed rather than a second setting: a tone step of a tenth of the range separates the object edges and strokes of the test images, which differ by 0.3 or more, from noise and paper grain, which the analysis's noise cut puts at 0.03, and a second control that only matters when the first is set would be one more thing to explain. The noise suggestion still offers Blur; offering Smooth instead is left for later.
