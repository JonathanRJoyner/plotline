# Ink fill rings as offsets of the outer ring

**Decided:** 2026-10-16

**Choice:** Amends the brief's "Ink style" row. The `ink` stage's outer ring is still the iso-line of the mask's distance transform a quarter pen inside the edge, now smoothed along a Gaussian of the smaller of one pen width (`RING_BLUR`) and two tone pixels (`PIXEL_BLUR`). In `fill` mode the inner rings are the iso-lines, at whole multiples of the spacing, of the distance transform of the cells the smoothed outer ring encloses (`raster::fill`, an even-odd scan-line fill), and they get only the least smoothing, a Gaussian of one working cell. Those iso-lines are the outer ring's offset curves (R. T. Farouki and C. A. Neff, "Analytic properties of plane offset curves", *Computer Aided Geometric Design* 7, 1990). Before, every ring was an iso-line of the mask's distance transform smoothed along a Gaussian of one pen width. Missed ink smaller than a square of `CRUMB` pens is still dropped. The stage's version goes to 2, as its output changed.

**Reason:** Smoothing every ring alike rounds each ring's corner by about the same amount, but an inward offset of a rounded corner has a smaller rounding radius, so the old rings parted along each corner's bisector and every ring pair left a hole there. The preview, which strokes paths at the pen's true width, showed these holes as dotted white lines along the ridges of filled shapes, and the crumb rule then dropped them. On an 8 mm square on a 0.05 mm tone map with a 0.5 mm pen, 272 of the square's 25,600 sample points lay more than half a pen from every path, the worst 0.40 mm from a line; now none does, with the same 10 paths (`a_square_fills_solid_into_its_corners`).

A throwaway measurement drew 8 shapes (squares turned 0, 4, 12 and 30 degrees, a disc, an annulus, a 60-degree triangle and an L) on tone maps of 0.05, 0.2 and 0.5 mm pixels, with 0.3 and 0.5 mm pens, and sampled every 0.05 mm. Points more than a pen inside a shape that no path covered fell from 6,981 to 1,026, points nearer the edge from 3,050 to 1,392, and paths from 648 to 608. On the 0.05 mm maps the four squares, the disc and the annulus now fill without a gap at both pen widths.

Alternatives, measured on the same shapes unless said otherwise:

- Less smoothing for the inner rings, without the second transform (the outer ring at one pen, the inner rings at one cell), also closed the corners (1,926 inner points), but the inner rings then wobbled on the tone map's pixel steps: on the 0.5 mm map with the 0.3 mm pen a disc's rings strayed from round by up to 0.54 pen, where the old rings reach 0.32 pen and the offsets 0.37. Pixel steps in the mask carry into every ring of its distance transform; offsets of one smoothed ring inherit only the working grid's half-cell steps.
- Smoothing the distance field once instead of the rings was not built: near a corner the field is the lesser of two planes, and blurring it makes each iso-line a copy of the last moved along the bisector rather than an offset, which leaves the same holes.
- One pen of smoothing on the outer ring rounded a filled square's own corners by more than the pen reaches on a fine map. Two tone pixels already iron out the pixel steps, and on coarse maps one pen stays the limit, as before.
- Inner levels at half a cell plus whole spacings, matching the outer ring's edge convention, put the deep rings about 0.4 cells further in, because a cell's distance to the nearest cell outside the ring overstates its distance to the ring by up to a cell near the ring and by less deeper in. That lost the 3 mm disc's tiny centre ring. Whole spacings keep the first inner ring at most a spacing from the outer one.
- Drawing every missed crumb that leaves a point more than half a pen from every ring closed more gaps but raised the path count by about half in a trial, mostly dots along the bisectors of corners sharper than about 74 degrees. There even exact offsets leave a hole at the default spacing: the gap along the bisector of a corner of angle `a` is `spacing / (1 + sin(a / 2))`, more than half a pen below 74 degrees at a spacing of 0.8. So crumbs stay dropped, as before.

What still leaves gaps, and is not a law for that reason: corners sharper than about 74 degrees, the middle of a shape whose depth falls between two rings, where the leftover sliver or centre is dropped as a crumb, and tone maps whose pixels are about as wide as the pen.
