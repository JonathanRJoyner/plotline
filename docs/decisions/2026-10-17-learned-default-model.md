# Learned lines draw with a built-in model

**Decided:** 2026-10-17

**Choice:** `learned` draws out of the box. Its `model.model` setting defaults to `xdog`, a model built into the engine that the `model` stage runs itself, with no runtime, no download and no licence screen, in the browser and natively alike. It is the XDoG of H. Winnemöller, J. E. Kyprianidis and S. C. Olsen, "XDoG: an eXtended difference-of-Gaussians compendium including advanced image stylization", *Computers & Graphics* 36(6), 2012, pp. 740-753: the luminance, placed and resized as the tone stage does it, is blurred at sigma and 1.6 sigma, sharpened as `S = (1 + p) G_sigma - p G_1.6sigma` (their eq. 7) and soft-thresholded at epsilon with a fixed sharpness phi of 10 (their eq. 6), and the line map is one minus that threshold. Three settings drive it and only it: `model.sigma` (1 px), `model.strength`, which is p (100), and `model.threshold`, which is epsilon (0.1). Trained models such as `teed-1` stay an opt-in download behind their licence screen, and a model file of your own still loads as before. Amends [learned line models](2026-10-16-learned-line-models.md), whose style needed a downloaded model.

The blurs are the tone stage's own (`math::blur`, the Gaussian below a sigma of 4 pixels and Kovesi's three boxes above; the brief's "Wide blur" row), so the engine module grew by 5,144 bytes, from 2,256,402 to 2,261,546 bytes as built locally, under its 2,300,000-byte limit.

With a model in place by default, three things change around the style. The picker lists the built-in model first, as "Lines (XDoG)", drawn on the current image like any style, and the style's description no longer says it needs a model. `Session::tone_match` returns nothing for a style with no tone stage, so the GUI, `plotline run` and the MCP `run` result show no tone error, SSIM or contrast for `learned`: its map holds lines, not the image's tones, so a drawing that follows it well would score as a poor tone match. The GUI hides Fit and Optimize for a style with no tone stage, as `learned` has no density setting to fit and no tone match to optimize. `learned` joins the regression gallery and the style guide's pictures, drawn at the built-in defaults. The style grid law now checks its cell too, but asks at least 1 % of the cell's pixels to be marked for a style with no tone stage, against 3 % for the others: outlines leave the areas between them blank, and the colour scene's outlines in a red pen marked 1.7 %.

**Reason:** The owner chose "Built-in + teed-1 opt-in" on 2026-10-17, so that a first-time user who picks `learned` gets a drawing with no download, no machine learning and no licence to accept. XDoG is a published, classical filter that needs nothing but two blurs, so it fits in the engine and carries no training data or weights whose terms need reading. The defaults were chosen on the code-rendered still life and colour scene: with p at 100 and epsilon near 0, the sharpened picture falls below the threshold only on the dark side of an edge, so the map holds outlines a pixel or two wide that the centreline tracer draws as one stroke each, closed around the spheres, the cube, the sun and the barn. The paper's lower p of about 20 with epsilon near the middle grey fills every area darker than epsilon, and the tracer drew the middle of each filled area instead of its outline. At a sigma of 0.8 pixels the outlines broke into more than twice as many pieces; at 1.5 they matched 1 with less detail. A flat picture no darker than the threshold has no edge to sharpen and draws nothing, which the law `the_built_in_line_model_draws_nothing_on_blank_paper` holds for any sigma and strength. The defaults were judged on code-made pictures only; a photograph's texture and noise, which a strength of 100 also sharpens, may want a larger sigma or a lower strength.
